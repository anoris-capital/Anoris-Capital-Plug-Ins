# Variance Commentary

## Overview
Drafts clear, specific written commentary explaining why key financial figures (NAV, expenses, fee income, specific line items) changed period-over-period — the narrative that accompanies financial reporting packages for investors or management.

## When to use this skill
- A period-end reporting package needs written commentary explaining significant variances.
- A user has a variance (budget vs. actual, or period vs. period) and needs it explained in clear prose.
- A user wants existing bullet-point variance notes turned into polished, reader-ready commentary.

## Inputs
- The financial data showing the variance (current period vs. prior period or vs. budget/expectation).
- Underlying detail on what drove the variance (specific transactions, one-time items, timing differences, market moves).
- Audience for the commentary (internal management vs. external investor reporting) since tone/detail level differs.

## Process
1. Identify which variances are significant enough to warrant commentary (material in dollar terms or as a meaningful percentage change) rather than commenting on every line item.
2. For each significant variance, identify the specific driver(s) — don't settle for a vague explanation like "market movements" if a more specific cause is available.
3. Quantify the driver's contribution to the variance where possible (e.g., "of the $X increase, $Y relates to the Q3 acquisition-related legal fees, and $Z relates to FX").
4. Write in clear, direct prose appropriate to the audience — external investor commentary should be more polished and less internal-jargon-heavy than a management-only note.
5. Avoid commentary that just restates the number ("expenses increased by $X") without explaining why — the driver is the point of the commentary.

## Output format
Short, specific written commentary organized by line item/category, each explaining the driver(s) of the variance in plain language, sized appropriately to the audience (concise for investor letters, more detailed for internal management packages).

## Notes / guardrails
- Commentary must be accurate to the underlying drivers — do not speculate on a cause that isn't supported by the data provided.
- Avoid vague, non-explanatory language; the whole point of variance commentary is specificity.
