# Competitive Analysis

## Overview
Benchmarks a target company against its direct and indirect competitors across financial performance, market position, product/strategy, and growth trajectory to answer "how does this company really stack up."

## When to use this skill
- A user wants to understand a company's competitive positioning ahead of a pitch, investment decision, or diligence process.
- A user wants a landscape view of a sector's key players before narrowing down a target list.
- A user wants to identify what differentiates a company from its peers (moat, pricing power, market share trends).

## Inputs
- Target company and its stated or inferred competitive set (direct competitors, adjacent players, potential disruptors).
- Dimensions to benchmark: financial (growth, margins, returns on capital), operational (market share, unit economics), and strategic (product breadth, geographic footprint, distribution).
- Time horizon (current snapshot vs. multi-year trend).

## Process
1. Confirm or build the competitive set — direct competitors first, then adjacent/indirect players worth noting.
2. Gather comparable financial metrics across the set (revenue growth, gross/operating margin, market share where available, capital efficiency).
3. Identify qualitative differentiators: business model differences, customer concentration, geographic mix, product breadth, pricing position (premium/value), distribution advantages.
4. Assess trend direction, not just current snapshot — is the target gaining or losing share/margin relative to peers over time?
5. Synthesize into a clear positioning narrative: where the company wins, where it's exposed, and what would need to be true for its position to improve or deteriorate.
6. Where relevant, connect the competitive analysis to a valuation implication (does the position justify a premium or discount multiple).

## Output format
A structured comparison (table or matrix) across the key dimensions, plus a short written synthesis of competitive positioning — strengths, vulnerabilities, and trend direction. Avoid a purely descriptive peer list with no analytical conclusion.

## Notes / guardrails
- Distinguish clearly between facts (reported financials, market share data) and analytical judgment (competitive positioning assessment).
- Note data limitations (e.g., private competitors with limited disclosure) rather than presenting estimates as hard facts.
