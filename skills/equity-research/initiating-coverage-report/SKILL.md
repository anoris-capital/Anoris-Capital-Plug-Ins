# Initiating Coverage Report

## Overview
Drafts a full initiating-coverage research report: business overview, industry/competitive context, financial analysis, valuation, investment thesis (bull/bear), and a rating/price target — the comprehensive first-look document that establishes coverage on a name.

## When to use this skill
- An analyst is starting coverage on a new name and needs the full report structure drafted.
- A user wants a comprehensive first-look writeup pulling together business, financial, and valuation analysis into one document.

## Inputs
- Company business description, segments, and geographic footprint.
- Historical financials and available forecasts/estimates.
- Industry and competitive context (peer set, market structure, growth drivers).
- Valuation approach(es) to use (comps, DCF, sum-of-the-parts).
- House view/rating conventions (e.g., buy/hold/sell, price target methodology, risk disclosures required).

## Process
1. Draft the business overview: what the company does, how it makes money, segment/geographic breakdown, and key products or services.
2. Draft the industry/competitive section: market structure, growth drivers, competitive dynamics, and the company's positioning (link to the competitive-analysis skill if a deeper benchmarking pass is needed).
3. Draft the financial analysis section: historical trends, margin drivers, balance sheet/capital structure, and capital allocation policy.
4. Build the valuation section using the agreed method(s) — trading comps and/or DCF — arriving at a price target range (link to the trading-comps and dcf-model skills as needed).
5. Draft the investment thesis: state the core bull case, core bear case, and the key variables that will determine which plays out.
6. Draft the rating and price target with explicit reasoning tying back to the valuation work.
7. Include a risks section explicitly listing what could break the thesis.
8. Ensure all required disclosures/disclaimers per house policy are included.

## Output format
A full report following house structure, typically: executive summary/thesis, business overview, industry context, financial analysis, valuation, risks, rating/price target, and disclosures.

## Notes / guardrails
- Investment ratings and price targets must be clearly reasoned from the analysis presented, not asserted.
- Always include a risks/bear-case section — a one-sided report is a compliance and credibility problem.
- Ensure required regulatory disclosures/disclaimers are present per house/firm policy.
