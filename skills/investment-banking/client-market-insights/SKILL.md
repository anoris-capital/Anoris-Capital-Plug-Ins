# Client & Market Insights

## Overview
Compiles a concise intelligence brief on a client or prospect: recent news, financial performance, strategic moves, leadership changes, and relevant market/sector developments — the "what do we need to know before we talk to them" package.

## When to use this skill
- Ahead of a client meeting, pitch, or relationship-coverage check-in.
- When a banker needs a fast catch-up on what's happened with a company or sector since the last touchpoint.
- When building the "recent developments" section of a pitch book.

## Inputs
- Client/prospect name and, if relevant, the specific deal or relationship context (e.g., M&A coverage, capital markets coverage, sector coverage).
- Lookback window (e.g., last quarter, last 12 months).
- Any specific angle the team cares about (refinancing needs, M&A activity, management changes, activist involvement).

## Process
1. Pull recent public news and disclosures: earnings releases, 8-Ks/major announcements, leadership changes, M&A activity, financing activity.
2. Pull recent financial performance highlights: last few quarters of revenue/margin trend, guidance changes, credit rating actions if applicable.
3. Scan for relevant sector/market developments that affect the client's strategic calculus (competitor moves, regulatory changes, macro factors).
4. Identify anything time-sensitive or actionable — an upcoming maturity, an activist stake, a stated strategic review — that the deal team should be aware of walking in.
5. Synthesize into a short, scannable brief rather than a raw news dump; lead with what's most relevant to the upcoming interaction.

## Output format
A one-to-two page brief: (1) headline items in bullet form, (2) recent financial snapshot, (3) relevant sector context, (4) suggested talking points or questions for the meeting. Cite sources for anything from public filings or news.

## Notes / guardrails
- Distinguish confirmed public information from speculation/rumor — label the latter clearly.
- Keep it tight and meeting-ready; this is a briefing document, not a research report.
